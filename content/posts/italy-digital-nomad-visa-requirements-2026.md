Italy's digital nomad visa is for non-EU citizens who do highly qualified work remotely, either self-employed (digital nomad) or for a company (remote worker). You need lawful income from that work of at least 24,789 euros a year (the current consular figure), health insurance, a registered lease in Italy and six months of experience. The permit lasts one year and is renewable.

This guide follows the [decree of 29 February 2024](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/id/2024/04/04/24A01716/SG) that created the visa, the Q&A of the Italian government's integration portal and the checklist of the Italian Consulate in Los Angeles. Consulates in other US cities publish their own lists, so always check the one that covers where you live.

## At a glance

* **Legal basis**: interministerial decree of 29 February 2024 (Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 79 of 4 April 2024), under article 27-quater of Legislative Decree 286/1998.
* **Who**: non-EU citizens doing highly qualified work remotely. No annual quota applies for stays over 90 days.
* **Income**: at least three times the healthcare co-payment exemption level, which the Los Angeles consulate gives as no less than **24,789 euros a year**.
* **Also required**: health insurance valid in Italy, housing documents, at least **six months** of experience in the work.
* **After arrival**: residence permit at the Questura within **eight working days**; it lasts up to **one year** and is renewable.

## Digital nomad or remote worker?

The decree uses one visa with two labels (article 2):

* **Digital nomad**: you work for yourself, as a freelancer, consultant or independent specialist, using technology that lets you work remotely.
* **Remote worker**: you are an employee or collaborator of a company, which can be based in Italy or abroad, and you do the job remotely.

Remote workers carry two extra requirements: a qualifying contract or binding offer, and a declaration from the employer (see below).

## Does your job count as "highly qualified"?

The visa is open only to work that meets article 27-quater(1) of the immigration code. The Los Angeles consulate lists three ways to show it:

1. a bachelor's, master's or doctorate degree, recognised through CIMEA or a Declaration of Value;
2. for regulated professions (architects, engineers, teachers and others), an attestation from the competent Italian authority under Legislative Decree 206/2007. Some professions need the Italian licence before you can apply;
3. at least **five years** of professional experience at a comparable level, documented for each job (company details, your position, contract or pay slips, a letter describing your duties and dates). For ICT executives and specialists the requirement drops to **three years** gained in the last seven.

## The requirements of the decree

Article 3 of the decree asks every applicant to:

1. have **lawful annual income** of at least three times the minimum level set for exemption from healthcare co-payment;
2. have **health insurance** for medical care and hospitalisation valid in Italy for the whole stay;
3. have **suitable documentation of housing**;
4. show at least **six months of prior experience** in the work they will do as a digital nomad or remote worker;
5. for remote workers only, present the **employment or collaboration contract**, or a binding offer, for highly qualified work.

### The income figure

The decree does not write a number in euros: it points to a parameter that can change. In its Q&A of 7 June 2024 the integration portal of the Italian ministries put the exemption level at 8,263.31 euros, so **24,789 euros** a year for an applicant without spouse or children. The Los Angeles consulate gives the same 24,789 euros "at the time of this writing". Ask your consulate for the figure that applies on the day you apply.

Two points from the Los Angeles checklist that often surprise Americans:

* the income **must come from the work you will do in Italy**. Social Security, rents or stocks are not considered;
* proof can be pay stubs, tax returns, W-2 forms or the three most recent bank statements.

If you plan to live on a pension or investments without working, this is not your visa: look at the elective residence visa instead.

### Extra requirements for remote workers

* A contract with a salary at least equal to the Italian national collective agreement for the job and, in any case, not below the ISTAT average annual salary. The integration portal put that average at about 33,500 euros in June 2024.
* A signed declaration from the employer, with a copy of the signer's ID, that the employer has had no convictions in the last five years for the offences listed in article 22(5-bis) of the immigration code (article 3(5) of the decree). The visa is refused or revoked if such a conviction exists.

Digital nomads do not need the provisional nulla osta of article 40(5) of DPR 394/1999, and remote workers do not need the work nulla osta of article 31 of the same DPR (article 3(2) and 3(3)).

## Documents a US consulate asks for

The Italian Consulate in Los Angeles lists, among others:

* a passport valid for at least **15 months** past your travel date, with two blank pages;
* proof that you live in its jurisdiction (driver's licence, state ID or utility bill) and, if you are not a US citizen, proof of legal US residence;
* the national visa form and one ICAO photo;
* insurance covering medical expenses, hospitalisation and repatriation, at least **30,000 euros** (or 50,000 dollars). The consulate calls inadequate coverage "the number one reason for visa rejections";
* a lease or deed in your name for the whole visa period. A lease must be a "contratto di locazione ad uso abitativo" registered with the Agenzia delle Entrate;
* proof of income and of six months' experience: tax returns, client invoices or professional memberships for digital nomads; tax returns, pay slips or an employer letter for remote workers;
* the visa fee, which the consulate updates every quarter.

Since 11 January 2025 every applicant for a national (D) visa is fingerprinted.

## Step by step

1. **Apply at the consulate** that covers your place of residence and wait for the visa.
2. **Enter Italy** with the visa.
3. **Within eight working days**, apply for the residence permit at the Questura of the province where you live (article 4(2) of the decree). Bring the documents you gave the consulate, stamped by the consulate. The integration portal says you can start working once the permit has been requested.
4. **Tax code**: the Questura generates your codice fiscale when it issues the permit (article 6(1)).
5. **VAT number**: digital nomads must also register for a partita IVA (article 6(2)).

The permit reads "nomade digitale – lavoratore da remoto", lasts up to one year and is renewed every year while the conditions remain (article 4(3)). The integration portal adds that under the general rule a permit is not renewed if you have been out of Italy for more than six months in a row without a serious, documented reason.

## Family members

The decree allows family reunification under article 29 of the immigration code. The Los Angeles consulate lists a spouse you are not separated from and children under 18; it says adult children and parents could not be sponsored at the time it wrote its page. Family members receive a permit for family reasons that lasts as long as yours. The request starts at the Questura that handled your permit.

## Social security and tax

**Social security.** Contributions follow Italian rules unless a bilateral agreement applies (article 5 of the decree). The United States and Italy have a totalization agreement: see our guide on [the US-Italy totalization agreement and remote work](/blog/us-italy-totalization-agreement-remote-work-2026/).

**Tax residence.** The visa does not decide where you pay tax. Under article 2 of the income tax code you are an Italian tax resident if, for most of the tax year, you have your civil residence, your domicile (where your personal and family ties are centred) or your physical presence in Italy; registration in the population registry is a presumption you can rebut. The new consolidated income tax code ([Legislative Decree 117/2026](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2026-06-19;117)), which applies from 1 January 2027, keeps the same tests in its article 2. If you are a US citizen, your US filing obligations continue: see our guide on the [foreign earned income exclusion](/blog/foreign-earned-income-exclusion-2027/).

If you become resident and work from Italy, it is worth asking a tax adviser whether the [impatriate regime](/blog/italy-impatriate-regime-2026-requirements-50-percent/) applies to you: it can halve the taxable part of income earned in Italy, but it has its own conditions.

## FAQ

### How much income do I need for the Italy digital nomad visa?

At least three times the minimum level for exemption from healthcare co-payment. The Italian Consulate in Los Angeles puts that at no less than 24,789 euros a year; the government's integration portal gives the same figure for an applicant without spouse or children.

### What is the difference between a digital nomad and a remote worker?

A digital nomad is self-employed; a remote worker is an employee or collaborator of a company in Italy or abroad. Remote workers also need a qualifying contract or binding offer and an employer declaration.

### Can I use Social Security, rent or investment income to qualify?

According to the Italian Consulate in Los Angeles, no: the income must come from the work you will do in Italy. If you plan to live on passive income, look at the elective residence visa.

### How long does the permit last?

Up to one year, renewable every year while the conditions remain. You apply at the Questura within eight working days of arriving.

### Can my family come with me?

Yes, under article 29 of the immigration code: the Los Angeles consulate lists a spouse and children under 18.

### Do I become an Italian tax resident?

Not automatically, but it is likely if you live in Italy for most of the year. The tests are in article 2 of the income tax code.
