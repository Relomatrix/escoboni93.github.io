DHS has proposed to remove the rule that makes USCIS defer to its own prior approval when it reviews an extension filed on Form I-129 for the same employer, worker and facts. If finalised, H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN and E extensions could be reviewed afresh, with more Requests for Evidence. Nothing has changed yet: comments close on 13 November 2026.

For other recent US changes, see our guides on the [proposed H-1B 60-day grace period rule](/blog/h1b-60-day-grace-period-proposed-rule-2026/) and the [end of automatic EAD extensions](/blog/ead-automatic-extension-ended-final-rule-2026/).

## TL;DR

* **Proposal**: delete **8 CFR 214.1(c)(5)**, the deference to prior approvals for Form I-129 extensions.
* **Status**: proposed rule published **14 October 2026** (91 FR 65119); comments close **13 November 2026**.
* **Scope**: all I-129 requests, including **H-1B, L-1, O-1, E and TN** extensions; about **208,429** petitions a year potentially eligible for deference.
* **Expected effect**: about **6,044** more RFEs a year; DHS found no change in denial rates in past data.
* **Today**: the current rule still applies until a final rule takes effect.

## What deference means today

The current regulation says that when USCIS adjudicates a Form I-129 involving the same parties and the same underlying facts, it gives deference to its prior determination of eligibility. Officers can depart from it only if there was a material error, a material change or new material information that adversely impacts eligibility. The policy was codified in December 2024, after being applied from 2004 to 2017 and reinstated in April 2021, according to the [proposed rule in the Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20941/removing-deference-to-prior-uscis-determinations-of-eligibility).

The [USCIS Policy Manual](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-a-chapter-4) currently tells officers who decide not to defer to acknowledge the prior approval and explain why in the RFE, notice of intent to deny or denial.

## What DHS proposes

DHS would remove paragraph (c)(5) and mark it "Reserved". The agency says this would keep the burden of proof on the petitioner, as required by INA section 291, and strengthen fraud detection. Officers could still consider a prior approval where relevant, but would no longer have to start from it.

| Item | Figure in the proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Form I-129 petitions potentially eligible for deference | 208,429 a year (FY 2022-2025 average) |
| Expected increase in RFE rate | 2.9 percentage points |
| Additional RFEs | about 6,044 a year |
| Extra decision time for petitions with an RFE | 64 days on average (FY 2022-2025) |
| Estimated cost to petitioners | about $133,987 a year |

DHS also notes that if an extension is filed on time and is still pending when status expires, the worker may remain in an authorised period of stay and may be eligible to keep working, for example under 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(20).

## What employers and workers can do now

* **File extensions early** and treat each one as a full application, with current evidence on the job, pay and the worker's maintenance of status.
* **Document changes**: if duties, worksite or business conditions have changed, explain them upfront rather than relying on the earlier approval.
* **Plan for RFEs** in project and travel timelines.
* **Comment before the deadline** if you want your view on record: regulations.gov, docket USCIS-2026-0529, before midnight Eastern time on 13 November 2026.

## FAQ

**What is the USCIS deference rule?**
A regulation that tells USCIS to defer to a prior I-129 approval involving the same parties and facts, unless there was a material error or change.

**Is the change already in force?**
No. It is a proposal; comments close on 13 November 2026.

**Which visas would be affected?**
All I-129 requests, including H-1B, L-1, O-1, E and TN extensions.

**Would more extensions be denied?**
DHS expects more RFEs, but found no change in denial rates in past data.

**How can I comment?**
On regulations.gov, docket USCIS-2026-0529, by 13 November 2026.
