From tax year 2027, people who move their tax residence to Italy can no longer combine the flat tax for new residents with the impatriate regime for workers. Article 2 of Decree-Law 38 of 27 March 2026 sets this rule, and Law 88 of 22 May 2026 converted it without changing that article.

This matters most for people with large foreign income who also plan to work in Italy, for example Americans relocating with a US portfolio and an Italian job. See also our guide to the [US-Italy totalization agreement](/blog/us-italy-totalization-agreement-remote-work-2026/).

## TL;DR

* **What changes**: the impatriate regime of Legislative Decree 209/2023 joins the reliefs that **cannot be combined** with the flat tax.
* **From**: people who transfer tax residence to Italy **from tax year 2027**.
* **Law**: Article 2 of Decree-Law 38/2026, converted by Law 88/2026.
* **Flat tax today**: EUR 300,000 a year on foreign income, plus EUR 50,000 per family member, for moves from 2026.
* **Impatriate regime**: 50% of Italian work income taxed, up to EUR 600,000 a year.

## What the law says

Article 2 amends paragraph 154 of Article 1 of Italy's 2017 budget law (Law 232/2016). In the consolidated text published in the Official Gazette, that paragraph now says the effects of the flat tax option cannot be combined with those of:

* Article 44 of Decree-Law 78/2010 (returning teachers and researchers);
* Article 16 of Legislative Decree 147/2015 (old impatriate regime);
* Article 5 of Legislative Decree 209/2023 (new impatriate regime), added by Decree-Law 38/2026.

Paragraph 2 limits the change to people who transfer their tax residence to Italy from tax year 2027.

## Why it changed

The research dossier of the Italian Chamber and Senate says the aim, stated in the government's explanatory report, is to restore the ban that already applied to the old impatriate regime. The 2023 regime had not been listed. The dossier also notes that the Revenue Agency, in ruling 159 of 2024, had allowed the impatriate regime for people who had opted for the flat tax, while barring both in the same tax year.

## The two regimes side by side

| | Flat tax for new residents | New impatriate regime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it covers | Income produced abroad | Employment, similar and self-employment income produced in Italy |
| How | Fixed substitute tax: EUR 300,000 a year (moves from 2026), EUR 50,000 per family member | 50% of income taxed (40% with a minor child), up to EUR 600,000 a year |
| Time abroad required | Not Italian resident for at least 9 of the previous 10 tax periods | Not resident in the previous 3 tax periods (6 or 7 if staying with the same group) |
| Length | Under the rules of Article 24-bis of the income tax code | Year of the move and the next four, with a commitment to stay 4 years |

Figures as reported in the parliamentary dossier on Decree-Law 38/2026.

## What to do if you move in 2027

If you plan to move your tax residence to Italy in 2027 or later and have both significant foreign income and Italian work income, you will need to choose one regime, because their effects can no longer be combined. The answer depends on how your foreign income compares with your Italian earnings. Italy's new consolidated income tax code (Legislative Decree 117/2026) also reorganises these rules, so check the version that applies to your year of arrival with an adviser.

## FAQ

**Can the two be combined from 2027?** No, for people moving their tax residence to Italy from tax year 2027.

**Am I affected if I moved in 2026?** The text applies only to moves from 2027.

**Is it final?** Yes, Decree-Law 38/2026 was converted by Law 88/2026 and Article 2 was not amended.

**How much is the flat tax?** EUR 300,000 a year for moves from 2026, plus EUR 50,000 per family member.
