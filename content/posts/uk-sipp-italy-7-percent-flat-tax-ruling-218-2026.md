Retiring from the UK to southern Italy with a Self-Invested Personal Pension? Italy's tax agency says SIPP payouts are foreign pension income, so they can open the door to the optional 7% flat tax on all foreign income. Ruling 218/2026 adds that withdrawal method and prior Italian property do not block access.

For more context, see our articles on [Italy's flat tax and impatriate regime in 2027](/blog/italy-flat-tax-impatriate-regime-no-combining-2027/), on [Italy's proposed exit tax](/blog/italy-exit-tax-2027-leaving-italy/) and on the [Italy digital nomad visa](/blog/italy-digital-nomad-visa-requirements-2026/).

## In short

* **The ruling**: Agenzia delle Entrate, **risposta n. 218/2026**, published **18 November 2026**.
* **The answer**: **SIPP benefits are pension income** (article 49(2)(a) TUIR), so a SIPP holder can access the **article 24-ter** regime.
* **Withdrawal method**: lump sum, drawdown or annuity **does not matter**.
* **Italian property**: owning homes or receiving rent in Italy **before the move** is **not an obstacle**.
* **Where**: small municipalities (up to **30,000** inhabitants) in eight southern regions or listed earthquake areas.
* **How long**: year of the move plus **nine** tax years.

## What is Italy's 7% regime for foreign pensioners

Under article 24-ter of the TUIR, as quoted in the ruling, individuals who receive **pension income paid by foreign entities** and move their tax residence to an eligible municipality can opt to pay a flat **7% substitute tax** on **income of any category produced abroad**, instead of ordinary progressive Italian income tax (IRPEF).

According to the [Agenzia delle Entrate guidance page](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/schede/agevolazioni/regime-opzionale-per-i-pensionati-esteri-che-cose-cittadini), the 7% applies for each tax year in which the option is valid. When you elect the regime you can also list foreign countries for which you do not want to apply the substitute tax.

## The case in ruling 218/2026

The applicant has lived in the UK continuously since 2000, owns a property in Italy that is let out and a share in two other Italian properties, and plans to move his tax residence to Italy in **2027 or 2028**. He holds a SIPP managed by a UK provider. In the application, the SIPP is described as:

* a **personal pension scheme** under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, with a provider supervised by the **Financial Conduct Authority**;
* a **registered pension scheme** with HM Revenue & Customs;
* funded by his own and employer contributions and possibly by transfers from other UK schemes;
* accessible, save for exceptions such as serious ill health, only from the **normal minimum pension age**.

He asked the Agency to confirm three points: that SIPP benefits qualify for the 7% regime, that the way they are withdrawn is irrelevant, and that his Italian properties and rental income before the move do not block the option.

## What the Agency decided

In [ruling no. 218/2026](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/10443592/Risposta+n.+218_2026/3dd9e126-690b-7d4f-72da-ed7d2cf997fd) the Agency recalls its earlier guidance:

* **Circular 21/E of 2020**: the regime covers pensions "of any kind" paid **exclusively by foreign entities**, including one-off payments such as capitalised pensions; people paid by an Italian pension institution are excluded.
* **Ruling 616/2021**: supplementary pensions paid by a foreign occupational fund or insurer, as capital or annuity, are pension income under article 49(2)(a).
* **Ruling 5/2024**: an earlier SIPP case, where the link to previous employment and the right to benefits only from a set pension age brought the SIPP within pension income.

Its conclusion: since SIPP benefits fall under article 49(2)(a), the applicant **can access the article 24-ter regime from the tax year in which he moves his tax residence to Italy**, "regardless of how the proceeds are withdrawn" and regardless of owning Italian property or receiving Italian rent in earlier years. The other conditions of the regime were **not assessed** in the ruling.

## The conditions you still need to meet

| Condition | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Income | Pension income paid by a **foreign** entity |
| Prior residence | Not tax resident in Italy in the **5 tax years** before the option takes effect |
| Country of origin | Moving from a country with **administrative cooperation agreements** with Italy |
| Location | Sicily, Calabria, Sardinia, Campania, Basilicata, Abruzzo, Molise, Puglia, or listed 2009/2016 earthquake municipalities |
| Population | Up to **30,000** inhabitants (from 7 April 2026; 20,000 before) |
| Duration | Year of the move plus the following **9** tax years |

The population cap was raised by [article 26 of Law 11 March 2026, no. 34](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/atto/serie_generale/caricaArticolo?art.codiceRedazionale=26G00050&art.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=2026-03-23&art.flagTipoArticolo=0&art.idArticolo=26&art.idGruppo=6&art.idSottoArticolo=1&art.idSottoArticolo1=10&art.progressivo=0&art.versione=1), which replaced "20,000" with "30,000".

## How to opt in

According to the [Agenzia delle Entrate page on electing the regime](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/schede/agevolazioni/regime-opzionale-per-i-pensionati-esteri/come-si-perfeziona-l-opzione-cittadini), the option is made in the **Italian tax return for the year of the move** and takes effect from that year. The return must state, among other things, non-residence in the previous five years, the last country of tax residence, any countries excluded from the regime, the country of the pension payer and the amount of foreign income subject to the 7% tax.

## What changes from 2027

[Legislative Decree 19 June 2026, no. 117](https://www.normattiva.it/eli/id/2026/07/03/26G00131/CONSOLIDATED), the new consolidated income tax code, applies from **1 January 2027** and repeals the old TUIR articles. The foreign pensioners' regime is carried over into **article 247**. If you plan to move in 2027 or later, check the text in force on Normattiva before deciding.

## Points to watch

* **Only foreign income**: the 7% applies to income produced abroad. Rent from Italian property stays under ordinary Italian rules.
* **The UK side**: the ruling deals with Italian tax only. In the application, the taxpayer noted that article 18 of the Italy-UK double tax treaty generally gives pension taxing rights to the state of residence; check how HMRC will treat your SIPP payments once you are Italian resident.
* **Rulings are case-specific**: an interpello answer applies to the facts described by that applicant.
