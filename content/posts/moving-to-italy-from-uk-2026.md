To move from the UK to Italy you need the right national visa for your purpose, a residence permit requested within 8 days of arrival, and registration with your comune if you stay more than 3 months. Your tax residence then shifts to Italy, and if you work you may qualify for the impatriate regime, which taxes only 50% of your Italian work income.

This guide puts the official rules from GOV.UK, the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Agenzia delle Entrate in the order you will meet them. If you are an Italian citizen coming home from the UK, the tax sections apply to you too.

## TL;DR

* UK citizens are non-EU nationals in Italy: check the visa you need with the Italian government's guided visa tool.
* Apply for a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) within 8 days of arriving, and register with the comune (iscrizione anagrafica) if you stay more than 3 months.
* From 2024, 183 days of physical presence, your domicile or your civil residence in Italy each make you Italian tax resident.
* The impatriate regime taxes 50% (or 40% with a child under 18) of Italian work income for 5 years, if you meet its conditions.
* You can drive on a UK licence for 12 months after becoming resident; then exchange it.

## Step 1: the visa

GOV.UK lists the purposes for which you can get an Italian visa: to work, including as a digital nomad or remote worker; to study; to live with family; to do research; to invest or do business; to live in Italy without working; or for religious activities. Which one fits you depends on your nationality, where you live, why you are going and for how long. The Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs has a guided tool, [Il visto per l'Italia](https://vistoperitalia.esteri.it/home/en), that asks four questions and tells you whether you need a visa and which one.

If you already lived in Italy before 1 January 2021, you and your family have rights under the Withdrawal Agreement, and GOV.UK recommends holding the biometric residence card (carta di soggiorno).

## Step 2: residence permit and registration

* **Residence permit.** As a non-EU national you must apply for a permesso di soggiorno within 8 days of arriving in Italy.
* **Registration with the comune.** If you plan to stay more than 3 months you register your residence at the Anagrafe of the comune where you live (iscrizione anagrafica). Dual UK-Italian nationals register as Italian nationals.
* **Schengen days.** With an Italian residence permit or long-stay visa, your time in Italy does not count towards the 90 visa-free days in the Schengen area. Legal residents with a permit or residence card do not need to register with the Entry/Exit System (EES).
* **Long term.** After at least 5 years of legal and continuous residence you can apply for permanent residence. Italy recognises dual citizenship with the UK.

## Step 3: when you become tax resident in Italy

Since the reform in Legislative Decree 209/2023, explained by the Agenzia delle Entrate in Circular 20/E of 4 November 2024, you are tax resident in Italy if, for most of the tax year, any one of these applies:

* your **civil residence** is in Italy;
* your **domicile** is in Italy, meaning the place where your personal and family relations are mainly centred;
* you are **physically present** in Italy: 183 days, or 184 in a leap year, counting part days;
* you are on the **register of residents** (anagrafe), which is a presumption that you can rebut.

GOV.UK also reminds UK nationals that Italian residents must also file the annual **Quadro RW** declaration of assets held abroad, such as UK bank accounts and investments, and that missing or wrong filings carry severe penalties.

## Step 4: the impatriate regime

If you move to work in Italy, check the impatriate regime (article 5 of Legislative Decree 209/2023). According to the Agenzia delle Entrate, from tax year 2024 it applies when all of these are true:

* you were not tax resident in Italy in the 3 tax years before the move (6 or 7 years if you keep working for the same employer or group);
* you commit to staying tax resident in Italy for at least 4 years;
* you work in Italy for most of the tax year;
* you have a high qualification or specialisation, as defined by Legislative Decrees 108/2012 and 206/2007.

If you qualify, only 50% of your Italian employment or self-employed professional income counts for tax, up to 600,000 euros a year, or 40% if you move with a child under 18. It lasts for the year of the move and the next four. Leave before 4 years and you repay the relief with interest. People who arrive from 2027 can no longer combine it with the flat tax for new residents: see [no combining from 2027](/blog/italy-flat-tax-impatriate-regime-no-combining-2027/).

## Step 5: double taxation and National Insurance

The [1988 UK-Italy Double Taxation Convention](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/italy-tax-treaties) has been in force since 31 December 1990 and applies in Italy from 1 January 1991 and to UK income tax from 6 April 1991. According to GOV.UK, it exists so that you do not pay tax on the same income in both countries.

If your UK employer sends you to Italy temporarily, you may stay in UK National Insurance for up to 2 years. You only keep paying in the UK if HMRC issues a **certificate of coverage**, which also shows the Italian authorities you do not owe contributions there. It is not a work permit. Self-employed people working temporarily in an EU country can also stay covered for up to 2 years.

## Step 6: your UK driving licence and car

* If you became resident in Italy on or after 1 January 2023, you can drive on your UK licence for up to 12 months.
* After 12 months you must exchange it for an Italian licence. In most cases you can exchange it without a test within 6 years of becoming resident. Withdrawal Agreement holders can exchange it at any time.
* You cannot drive a foreign-registered car once you have been resident for 90 days or more: register it with Italian plates within 90 days of becoming resident.

## What to check on your own case

The order of these steps is fixed, but the details depend on your situation: which visa, when your tax residence starts, whether the impatriate regime applies, and how the treaty treats your pension or UK investments. ## FAQ

### Do UK citizens need a residence permit to live in Italy?

Yes. Since Brexit, UK citizens are non-EU nationals in Italy. According to GOV.UK, a non-EU national must apply for a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) within 8 days of arriving in Italy.

### When do I become tax resident in Italy?

From 2024 you are tax resident in Italy if, for most of the tax year, you have your civil residence, your domicile (the centre of your personal and family relations) or your physical presence in Italy. Being on the register of residents (anagrafe) creates a presumption of residence that can be rebutted. Physical presence means 183 days, or 184 in a leap year, counting part days.

### Can I keep driving on my UK licence in Italy?

If you became resident on or after 1 January 2023, you can drive on your UK licence for up to 12 months. After that you must exchange it for an Italian licence, which in most cases you can do without a test within 6 years of becoming resident.

### Is there a tax break for people moving to Italy?

Yes. Under the impatriate regime, workers who move their tax residence to Italy and meet the conditions are taxed on only 50% of their Italian work income, up to 600,000 euros a year, or 40% with a child under 18. They need at least 3 years of tax residence abroad and must stay 4 years.

### Will I pay tax twice, in the UK and in Italy?

The UK and Italy have a double taxation convention, signed in 1988 and in force since 31 December 1990, so that the same income is not taxed in both countries. You claim relief under the treaty through the tax authorities.
