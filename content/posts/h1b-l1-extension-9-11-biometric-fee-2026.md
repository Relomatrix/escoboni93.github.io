If your employer is a covered employer, H-1B and L-1 extensions now cost more: since September 9, 2026, the 9-11 Biometric Fee of $4,000 (H-1B) or $4,500 (L-1) applies to every extension of status, even when the same employer files for the same worker. Amended petitions without an extension remain exempt.

For the wider context, see our pillar on [cross-border employment](/blog/cross-border-employment/), our piece on the [USCIS deference rule for H-1B extensions](/blog/uscis-deference-rule-h1b-extensions-2026/) and the [proposed H-1B 60-day grace period changes](/blog/h1b-60-day-grace-period-proposed-rule-2026/).

## TL;DR

* **Rule**: DHS final rule "9-11 Response and Biometric Entry-Exit Fee for H-1B and L-1 Visas", [91 FR 51360](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-16231/9-11-response-and-biometric-entry-exit-fee-for-h-1b-and-l-1-visas), published August 10, 2026, **effective September 9, 2026**.
* **Amounts** (set by statute): **$4,000** per H-1B petition, **$4,500** per L-1 petition.
* **Who pays**: only **covered employers**, with 50+ US employees and more than 50% of them in H-1B or L-1 status.
* **What's new**: the fee now applies to **all extensions**, not only initial petitions and changes of employer.
* **Form**: the current Form I-129 edition is **09/09/26**; USCIS may reject forms with pages from different editions.

## What the final rule changes

Congress created the 9-11 Biometric Fee in Public Law 114-113 (2015). For years DHS read the law as requiring the fee only when the separate $500 Fraud Prevention and Detection Fee also applied: initial H-1B or L-1 petitions and change-of-employer petitions. Extensions filed by the same employer for the same worker did not pay it.

The final rule says that reading "was not the best one". It amends 8 CFR 106.2(c)(8) and (9) so that covered employers pay the fee on:

| Petition filed by a covered employer | Before Sept 9, 2026 | From Sept 9, 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial H-1B or L-1 petition | Fee due | Fee due |
| Change of employer (with or without extension) | Fee due | Fee due |
| Extension, same employer, same worker | Not due | **Fee due** |
| Amended petition without extension | Not due | Not due |

DHS estimates that, had the rule applied in fiscal years 2018-2025, the share of covered employers' H-1B petitions paying the fee would have risen from **27% to 75%**. The rule also notes that, under the current congressional extension, the fee is set to expire on **September 30, 2027**.

## Are you a covered employer?

According to the [USCIS Form I-129 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-129) and the [H and L filing fees page](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/h-and-l-filing-fees-for-form-i-129-petition-for-a-nonimmigrant-worker), a petitioner is covered if **both** are true:

1. it employs **50 or more** individuals in the United States; and
2. **more than 50%** of those employees are in **H-1B, L-1A or L-1B** status.

The final rule adds the words "in the aggregate" to confirm that DHS counts all full-time and part-time employees in those statuses. Most employers, including most small and mid-size companies hiring a few H-1B workers, are **not** covered and do not pay this fee. Other I-129 fees (base filing fee, ACWIA fee, Fraud fee, Asylum Program Fee) follow their own rules on the [USCIS fee schedule (G-1055)](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055).

## Filing checklist for HR teams

* **Check the edition**: USCIS lists **09/09/26** as the current Form I-129 edition and warns that it may reject a form with missing pages or pages from a different edition.
* **Check the date**: petitions postmarked or electronically submitted on or after **September 9, 2026** must include the fees required by the final rule.
* **Recalculate covered status** before each filing, using the aggregate headcount.
* **Budget extensions**: for covered employers, each H-1B extension now adds $4,000 and each L-1 extension $4,500.
* **Do not pass it on to the worker**: the regulations require the employer to pay, and H-1B employers are generally barred from recouping petition fees through wage cuts, as DHS notes in the rule.

## And the $100,000 H-1B payment?

This is a separate measure. The [USCIS Form I-129 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-129) states that on **June 8, 2026** the US District Court for the District of Massachusetts vacated the agency guidance implementing the $100,000 payment for certain H-1B petitions (*State of California v. Mullin*), and that on **July 24, 2026** the First Circuit denied the government's motion to stay. DHS says it disagrees but will comply while it considers next steps, and that it still plans to collect the payment if the order is lifted.

## FAQ

**What is the 9-11 Biometric Fee?**
A statutory $4,000 (H-1B) or $4,500 (L-1) fee paid by covered employers.

**What changed on September 9, 2026?**
It now applies to all extensions, including same-employer extensions.

**Which employers are covered?**
Those with 50+ US employees and more than 50% in H-1B or L-1 status.

**Do amended petitions pay it?**
Not if they do not request an extension.

**Is it retroactive?**
No. DHS says it does not apply to past or pending petitions.

**What about the $100,000 payment?**
