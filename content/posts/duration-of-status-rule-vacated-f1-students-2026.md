Duration of status survives. On 16 November 2026 a federal judge in Massachusetts vacated the Department of Homeland Security rule that would have given F-1 students, J-1 exchange visitors and I media workers fixed admission periods. The rule never took effect, so students and scholars stay under the long-standing rules and need no new filing.

If you are planning a move to the US for study or work, see also our articles on the [proposed 60-day H-1B grace period](/blog/h1b-60-day-grace-period-proposed-rule-2026/), on the [end of automatic EAD extensions](/blog/ead-automatic-extension-ended-final-rule-2026/) and on [USCIS deference for H-1B extensions](/blog/uscis-deference-rule-h1b-extensions-2026/).

## In short

* **The ruling**: *Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration v. DHS*, No. 26-13799-FDS (D. Mass.), memorandum and order of **16 November 2026**.
* **The outcome**: the final rule published on **17 July 2026** (**91 FR 44976**) is **vacated in its entirety** under the Administrative Procedure Act, 5 U.S.C. § 706.
* **What stays**: admission for **duration of status** (D/S) for F and J visa holders, with the existing rules on I-20 and DS-2019 extensions, OPT, STEM OPT and transfers.
* **What does not apply**: the **four-year cap**, the **USCIS extension of stay**, the shorter **30-day** F-1 departure period and the new limits on transfers and changes of program.
* **Next step**: DHS may appeal and seek a stay, so the situation can still change.

## What duration of status means

Under duration of status, F-1 students and J-1 exchange visitors are not admitted until a fixed date. Their Form I-94 shows "D/S", and they can stay as long as they keep their status: a full course of study for students, the program on the DS-2019 for exchange visitors, plus any authorised practical training. When more time is needed, the school or sponsor extends the I-20 or DS-2019 without an application to USCIS.

## What the July 2026 rule would have changed

The final rule, titled "Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors, and Representatives of Foreign Information Media", was published in the [Federal Register on 17 July 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant) with an effective date of **15 September 2026**. According to the [Student and Exchange Visitor Program announcement](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2026/07/dhs-publishes-final-rule-establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of), it would have:

| Topic | Duration of status (still in force) | Vacated July 2026 rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Admission | Until the program and authorised training end | Program length on the I-20, **max 4 years**, plus 30 days to arrive and 30 days to depart |
| More time needed | School extends the I-20 | **Extension of stay with USCIS**, or leave and re-enter |
| OPT and STEM OPT | Covered by D/S | Extension of stay needed if beyond the admission date |
| F-1 departure period after completion | **60 days** | **30 days** |
| Transfers and new programs | Existing rules | Graduate students barred from transferring and from changing educational objectives, save exceptions; no new program at the same or a lower level |
| I visas (foreign media) | Duration of status | Fixed periods of up to **240 days** in most cases |

The rule also set a transition: F students admitted under D/S before 15 September 2026 could stay until the program end date on their I-20, or the end of OPT, but not beyond **14 November 2030**.

## What the court decided

On 14 September 2026 the court had already postponed the rule's effective date with a preliminary injunction. In its [memorandum and order of 16 November 2026](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mad.305059/gov.uscourts.mad.305059.73.0.pdf), Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV granted summary judgment to the plaintiffs, a coalition of higher education associations and labour unions, and denied the government's motion.

The court adopted its earlier finding that DHS had failed to weigh costs and benefits, that fraud and national security concerns did not support the rule, that less burdensome alternatives, including better use of the SEVIS reporting system, were not considered, and that significant comments were not adequately answered. It also held that the limits on academic programs exceeded DHS's statutory authority over the F-1 classification. As a remedy, it rejected remand without vacatur, noting that "the current regime has existed for decades, and the Final Rule has not gone into effect", and **vacated the rule**.

## What this means if you study or work in the US

* **No action is needed because of the ruling**: there is no USCIS extension of stay to file under the vacated rule.
* **Your I-94 stays "D/S"**: keep your I-20 or DS-2019 valid and follow your school's or sponsor's rules on extensions and transfers.
* **Maintenance of status still matters**: a full course of study, authorised employment only and timely SEVIS reporting through your designated school official or responsible officer.
* **Watch for an appeal**: DHS may appeal and request a stay. Check the official announcements of the Student and Exchange Visitor Program before travelling or changing program.
* **Related changes are separate**: other proposals, such as new fees for optional practical training, follow their own rulemaking and are not decided by this judgment.
