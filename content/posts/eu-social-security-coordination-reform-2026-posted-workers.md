The revised EU rules on social security coordination entered into force on 1 November 2026, but most of them apply only from 1 November 2028. Regulation (EU) 2026/2359 tightens postings (three months of prior home cover, a two-month break after 24 months, prior notification) and updates unemployment, family and long-term care benefits.

The regulation amends Regulation (EC) No 883/2004 and its implementing Regulation (EC) No 987/2009, the two texts that decide which country's social security system covers people who live, work or look for a job across EU borders. Here is what it changes, based on the official texts and the European Commission's summary.

## TL;DR

* Regulation (EU) 2026/2359 was published in the Official Journal on 27 October 2026 and entered into force on 1 November 2026.
* Most new rules, including applicable legislation and prior notification, apply from 1 November 2028.
* Posted workers will need at least three months of home-country affiliation before a posting.
* After 24 months of posting, a break of at least two months is required before a new posting.
* Postings must be notified in advance, except business trips and postings of up to three days (not in construction).

## What is EU social security coordination?

Each EU country designs its own social security system: which benefits exist, who is eligible and what contributions are due. EU coordination rules do not harmonise those systems. They decide which single country's legislation applies to a person who moves, so that nobody is left without cover or pays contributions in two countries at once. According to the European Commission, about 16 million EU citizens live or work in another EU country.

The two-year gap is meant to let member states adapt their national procedures.

## Posted workers: the new conditions

The Commission lists four changes for postings, presented as part of "fairer labour mobility" together with a new definition of fraud in social security coordination:

1. **Prior affiliation of three months.** Workers must be affiliated to social security in their home country for at least three months before being posted to another EU country.
2. **Break after 24 months.** After 24 months of posting, a break of at least two months is needed before another posting.
3. **Prior notification.** Postings must be notified before they happen.
4. **Exemptions from notification.** Business trips are excluded, and so are short-term postings of a maximum of three days in all sectors except construction.

For employers, this mainly means planning: checking the hiring date of staff to be sent abroad, tracking cumulative posting periods and building notification into the assignment workflow before 2028.

## Working in two or more countries

The reform also clarifies which social security legislation applies to postings and to people who work in two or more EU countries. This matters for hybrid and remote set-ups where an employee splits working time between countries. Our guide to [cross-border employment risks](/blog/cross-border-employment/) explains why the social security question is separate from tax residence.

## Unemployment, family and long-term care benefits

According to the Commission, the regulation:

* creates a coherent regime for coordinating **long-term care benefits**;
* introduces new rules on **family benefits**;
* sets new arrangements for **unemployment benefits** in cross-border cases and extends the period during which a jobseeker can move to another country while keeping unemployment benefit entitlement.

These rules too apply from 1 November 2028.

## Stronger cooperation between authorities

National authorities will exchange more information on the social security status of people working in another country, with clearer procedures and deadlines when there are doubts about the validity of documents, and new tools against fraud and errors.

## What to do now

1. Map who in your organisation is posted or works in more than one EU country.
2. Check when each posted worker joined the home social security system.
3. Track posting durations against the future 24-month limit and two-month break.
4. Plan a prior-notification step for assignments starting from November 2028.

## FAQ

### When did the revised EU social security coordination rules enter into force?

Regulation (EU) 2026/2359 was published in the Official Journal on 27 October 2026 and entered into force on 1 November 2026. According to the European Commission, most of the updated rules will only apply from 1 November 2028.

### How long must a worker be insured at home before being posted?

Workers must be affiliated to social security in their home country for at least three months before being posted to another EU country.

### Is there a limit on back-to-back postings?

Yes. After 24 months of posting, there must be a break of at least two months before another posting.

### Do business trips need prior notification?

No. Business trips are excluded from prior notification, as are short-term postings of up to three days, except in construction.

### Do I need to change anything today?

Most obligations apply from 1 November 2028. Until then, the current rules remain the reference, so now is the time to review posting policies and documentation.
