A USCIS policy memo dated November 4, 2026 (PM-602-0201) tells officers how to weigh public benefits and family support when judging good moral character for naturalization. Lawful benefit use is not a bar, but benefit fraud, unpaid support for dependents and unmet Affidavit of Support duties can lead to a denial of Form N-400.

## TL;DR

* **What**: USCIS [Policy Memorandum PM-602-0201](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/policy-alerts/PM-602-201-GMCPublicBenefits-20261104.pdf), "Good Moral Character Determination: Failure to Support Dependents, Public Benefits, and Sponsorship through Affidavit or Declaration of Support", dated **November 4, 2026**.
* **Who**: people applying for US citizenship on **Form N-400**, and sponsors who signed Forms I-864, I-864EZ, I-864A, I-134 or I-134A.
* **Key line**: lawful receipt of public benefits is **not a bar** to naturalization; fraud, willful non-support and broken support commitments can be.
* **Next**: USCIS will update the **Policy Manual** and **revise Form N-400** to ask about benefit use and support of dependents.

## What the memo says

Every naturalization applicant must show good moral character (GMC) during the statutory period. Under [8 CFR 316.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-316/section-316.10), USCIS judges GMC case by case against "the standards of the average citizen in the community of residence", and can also look at conduct before that period.

According to the memo, an applicant may be found to lack GMC if he or she has:

* **willfully failed or refused to support dependents**;
* been convicted of, or admitted, an offense involving **fraud or deception** in obtaining public benefits;
* committed such a fraud offense, even **without a conviction**;
* **failed Form I-864, I-864EZ or I-864A obligations** during the period of enforceability;
* **failed Form I-134 or I-134A commitments** while they were in effect;
* received **means-tested public benefits** for a reason the community would see as **intentional avoidance of self-sufficiency**.

## Public benefits: what is and is not a problem

The memo keeps a clear line between lawful use and misconduct:

| Situation | How the memo treats it |
| --- | --- |
| Lawful receipt of public benefits | Not a bar to naturalization and not always a negative factor |
| False income, household size or other data on a benefit application | Possible fraud, can preclude GMC even without a conviction |
| Not reporting disqualifying changes (including long trips abroad that affect eligibility, e.g. SSI) | Listed as benefit-related fraud |
| Cutting work hours only to stay under benefit thresholds | May be adverse to GMC after an individualized analysis |
| Dependents relying on means-tested benefits | May show failure to support them, depending on circumstances |

The memo says any adverse finding must be tied to **specific conduct**, "rather than poverty, disability, or lawful benefit receipt", and must weigh the reason for benefit use, efforts toward self-sufficiency and positive contributions.

## Sponsors and the Affidavit of Support

Under [INA 213A](https://www.uscis.gov/laws-and-policy/legislation/immigration-and-nationality-act), Form I-864 is a legally enforceable contract: the sponsor must keep the immigrant at an income of at least **125% of the federal poverty line** during the enforceability period. The memo says that if a sponsor, a household member who signed Form I-864A, or a supporter on Form I-134/I-134A does not meet those commitments, especially when the sponsored person receives means-tested benefits, this can count as a **negative factor** when that sponsor later applies for citizenship.

## What changes in practice

* **New questions**: USCIS will revise Form N-400 to collect information on public benefit use and support of dependents.
* **Closer interviews**: after the new Policy Manual guidance is published, officers will question applicants on these points.
* **Child support abroad counts**: the memo notes that compliance with support obligations in the United States or abroad may be considered.
* **Internal guidance**: the memo is guidance for USCIS staff and does not create enforceable rights.

## FAQ

**What is PM-602-0201?**
A USCIS policy memo of November 4, 2026 on good moral character, public benefits and support obligations in naturalization cases.

**Does receiving public benefits block citizenship?**
No. Lawful receipt is not a bar; officers must look at specific conduct case by case.

**What conduct can count against me?**
Benefit fraud, willful failure to support dependents, and unmet I-864, I-864A, I-134 or I-134A commitments.

**Will Form N-400 change?**
Yes, USCIS plans to add questions on benefit use and support of dependents.

**How far back can USCIS look?**
Mainly the statutory period (five years under the general rule), and earlier conduct when relevant.
