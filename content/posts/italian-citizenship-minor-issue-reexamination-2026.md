The minor issue is over for most cases. On 26 July 2026 Italy's Supreme Court, in Joint Sections, ruled that a parent's foreign naturalisation during a child's minority does not break the line of Italian citizenship for children born dual nationals. Applicants refused on that ground can request a re-examination of the original application, not file a new one.

## In short

* **Ruling**: Italian Supreme Court, Joint Sections, judgment **no. 24045 of 26 July 2026**, reversing order no. 17161/2023.
* **Ministry guidance**: Ministry of the Interior **circular no. 65050 of 10 August 2026** tells offices to apply the new principles.
* **Who is covered**: descendants whose cohabiting Italian parent naturalised abroad between **1 July 1912 and 15 August 1992** while they were minors.
* **After a denial**: request a **re-examination** of the original application from the office that refused it; do not file a new one.
* **Consulates are acting**: for example, the Italian consulate in Adelaide said on **18 November 2026** that it is re-examining refused applications and will notify applicants; the Consulate General in San Francisco has published a re-examination request form.

## What the minor issue was

Under Law 555/1912, a child born abroad to an Italian parent in a country that grants citizenship by birth on its soil (*ius soli*), such as the United States, acquired both citizenships at birth. In 2023 the First Civil Section of the Supreme Court (order no. 17161/2023) held that, if the cohabiting parent then naturalised in the foreign country while the child was still a minor, the child also lost Italian citizenship under article 12 of the same law, unless the parent later reacquired it.

The Ministry of the Interior adopted that reading in its circular of **3 October 2024, no. 43347**, and applications built on such lines were refused.

## What the Supreme Court decided in 2026

According to [circular no. 65050](https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/documentazione/circolari/circolare-n65050-del-10-agosto-2026-cittadinanza-iure-sanguinis-rapporto), the Joint Sections held in judgment no. 24045 of 26 July 2026 that article 7 of Law 555/1912 governs dual nationality from birth on its own, and that losing Italian citizenship in that case requires a voluntary and informed choice. The principles that offices must now apply are:

1. a minor born abroad to an Italian father or mother in a *ius soli* country is a **dual national by law from birth** and keeps Italian citizenship even if the parent naturalises or loses it, unless an international treaty provides otherwise or the child renounces it after coming of age;
2. article 12 applies only to a minor who holds **Italian citizenship alone**;
3. the full equality of mothers and fathers, following Constitutional Court judgments no. 87/1975 and no. 30/1983, also applies to the effects of a parent's loss of citizenship on the child.

## Which naturalisation dates are covered

| Date of the parent's foreign naturalisation | Rule applied, per circular 65050 |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 30 June 1912 | Principles of order no. 454/2024 on the 1865 Civil Code still apply, pending a different Supreme Court ruling |
| 1 July 1912 to 15 August 1992 | New principles of judgment no. 24045/2026: the line is **not** broken |
| From 16 August 1992 | Outside the scope of the ruling (Law 555/1912 no longer in force) |

## How re-examination works after a denial

The circular sets out these steps for applications already refused:

* **No new application**: it would be assessed under the rules in force today.
* **Re-examination only**: if the denial was based solely on the old reading of articles 7 and 12, a new decision can be issued under the 2026 ruling.
* **Rules at the time of filing**: the new decision applies the legal framework in force when the original application was submitted.
* **Same office**: the authority that issued the denial is competent, even if you have since moved within Italy or abroad.
* **2025 limits**: if the original application was filed **before 27 March 2025** on the basis of an appointment communicated by that date, the limits of article 3-bis of Law 91/1992 (decree-law 28 March 2025, no. 36, converted by Law 23 May 2025, no. 74) do not apply.

Consulates do not all work the same way. The [Italian consulate in Adelaide](https://consadelaide.esteri.it/en/news/dal_consolato/2026/11/italian-citizenship-by-descent-iure-sanguinis-update-on-the-so-called-minor-issue-re-examination-of-applications/) announced on 18 November 2026 that it is re-examining refused applications and will notify the people concerned directly. The [Consulate General in San Francisco](https://conssanfrancisco.esteri.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/formulario_riconsiderazione_minori_jure_sanguinis_pubblicato_2026.pdf) instead published a request form: it asks for a copy of the denial, a copy of a valid passport, a power of attorney if a representative files it, and a signature notarised by a Notary Public. The form states that a re-examination does not imply recognition and that all requirements will be checked again.

## What to do now

* **Read your denial**: check whether the only ground was the parent's naturalisation during your minority.
* **Check the date**: the naturalisation must fall between 1 July 1912 and 15 August 1992.
* **Find the right office**: it is the consulate or comune that refused you, not the one nearest to you today.
* **Check that office's website**: some re-examine on their own initiative, others want a written request.
* **Keep your filing date proof**: it decides whether the 2025 limits apply to you.

## FAQ

**What was the minor issue?** The reading under which a parent's naturalisation during the child's minority broke the line of descent, adopted by the Ministry in October 2024.

**Is it over?** For naturalisations between 1 July 1912 and 15 August 1992, yes: judgment 24045/2026 and circular 65050 say the line is not broken.

**Do I reapply?** No, request a re-examination from the office that denied you.

**Do the 2025 limits apply?** Not if the original application was filed before 27 March 2025 on the basis of an appointment communicated by then.

*General information, not legal advice. Every citizenship case depends on the family records: check your case with the competent consulate or comune, or with a qualified professional.*
