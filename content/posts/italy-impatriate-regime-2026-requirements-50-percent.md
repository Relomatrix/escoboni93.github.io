Italy's impatriate regime cuts income tax for people who move their tax residence to Italy after at least three years abroad: work income earned in Italy, up to 600,000 euros a year, is taxed on only half its amount, or 40% with a minor child. It lasts five years and you must stay at least four.

It is open to Italians coming home and to foreigners, Americans included. This guide follows the text of [article 5 of Legislative Decree 209/2023](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2023-12-27;209) and of article 225 of the new income tax code that replaces it from 2027. An Italian version is linked at the top of the page.

## At a glance

* **Who**: people who move their tax residence to Italy **from 2024 onwards** and were not Italian tax residents in the previous **three years**.
* **Tax break**: work income earned in Italy counts for **50%**, up to **600,000 euros** a year; **40%** with a minor child.
* **Which income**: employment income, similar income and self-employment income from professional activities.
* **How long**: the year you move plus **four years**. Leave before four years and you repay the benefit with interest.
* **From 2027**: the regime moves to article 225 of the new income tax code, same rules; it can no longer be combined with the flat tax for new residents.

## The four conditions

Article 5(1) requires **all** of these:

1. **Commitment to stay**: you commit to remain an Italian tax resident for at least four years.
2. **Time abroad**: you were not an Italian tax resident in the three tax years before the move (more if you keep the same employer, see below).
3. **Work in Italy**: you work in Italy for most of the tax year.
4. **High qualification**: you meet the high qualification or specialisation requirements of Legislative Decrees 108/2012 and 206/2007, or you have done research, including applied research, in artificial intelligence technologies.

### If you keep the same employer

If in Italy you work for the employer you had abroad, or for a company of its group, the time abroad becomes:

* **six years**, if you had not worked in Italy for that employer or group before leaving;
* **seven years**, if you had worked in Italy for that employer or group before leaving.

The same group means companies linked by direct or indirect control, or controlled by the same entity (article 5(2)). This is the typical case of an intra-company transfer.

## What it means in numbers

The regime does not change the tax rates: it reduces the part of your income they apply to.

| Gross annual salary earned in Italy | Basic rule (50%) | With a minor child (40%) |
|---|---|---|
| 100,000 euros | 50,000 euros count as income | 40,000 euros count as income |
| 200,000 euros | 100,000 euros count as income | 80,000 euros count as income |

The 40% rate applies if you move with a minor child, or if a child is born or adopted while you use the regime, from that year onwards. The child must be resident in Italy (article 5(4) and (5)).

## Duration and clawback

The regime applies from the tax year you move and for the four following years: **five years** in total. If you do not keep Italian tax residence for at least four years, you lose the benefit and must repay what you received, with interest (article 5(3)).

## What changes in 2027

**The regime stays.** [Legislative Decree 117/2026](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2026-06-19;117), the new consolidated income tax code, applies from 1 January 2027 (article 377). The impatriate regime becomes its **article 225**, with the same conditions, percentages and 600,000 euro cap.

**No combining with the new residents' flat tax.** For people who move their tax residence to Italy from 2027, the impatriate regime can no longer be added to the flat tax on foreign income. See [Italy flat tax and impatriate regime: no combining from 2027](/blog/italy-flat-tax-impatriate-regime-no-combining-2027/).

## Notes for Americans

* **You still file in the US.** The regime lowers Italian tax only. Your US return continues, and the foreign tax credit or the [foreign earned income exclusion](/blog/foreign-earned-income-exclusion-2027/) interact with a lower Italian tax bill: ask a cross-border adviser to run both sides.
* **Your visa is a separate question.** If you will work remotely from Italy, see our guide to the [Italy digital nomad visa](/blog/italy-digital-nomad-visa-requirements-2026/).
* **Social security** follows the US-Italy totalization agreement: see [the agreement and remote work](/blog/us-italy-totalization-agreement-remote-work-2026/).

## Before you move

* **Count your years abroad** since you were last an Italian tax resident, if you ever were, and check whether you keep the same employer or group.
* **Gather the evidence**: foreign tax residence certificates, contracts, proof of qualification.
* **Check your qualification**: degree or regulated profession, or research in artificial intelligence.
* **Talk to your employer or accountant** about how to claim the regime for your type of income.

## FAQ

### How much does the impatriate regime save?

Work income earned in Italy, up to 600,000 euros a year, counts for only 50%, or 40% with a minor child resident in Italy. On a 100,000 euro salary, 50,000 euros enter the income on which Italian income tax is calculated, or 40,000 with a child.

### Can a US citizen or other foreigner use it?

Yes, if you move your tax residence to Italy and meet the conditions: three years without Italian tax residence, work in Italy for most of the year, a four-year commitment and a high qualification.

### How many years abroad do I need?

At least three. Six or seven if you work in Italy for the same employer or group you had abroad.

### How long does it last?

Five years: the year you move and the four following. Leave before four years and you repay the benefit with interest.

### Does the regime still exist from 2027?

Yes, in article 225 of the new income tax code, with the same rules. From 2027 it cannot be combined with the flat tax for new residents.
