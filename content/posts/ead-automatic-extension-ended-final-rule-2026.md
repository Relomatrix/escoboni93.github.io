DHS has made it final: since October 14, 2026, renewal EAD applications filed on or after October 30, 2025 no longer extend an expiring work permit automatically. The old up-to-540-day extension survives only for renewals filed before that date, plus TPS cases covered by law or a Federal Register notice.

This guide explains the final rule in plain terms for workers and employers. If you are new to working across borders, start with our [cross-border employment guide](/blog/cross-border-employment/).

## TL;DR

* The final rule (USCIS docket USCIS-2025-0271) took effect on October 14, 2026 and adopts the October 30, 2025 interim final rule without changes.
* Renewal EAD applications filed on or after October 30, 2025 get no automatic extension.
* Renewals filed before October 30, 2025 keep the up-to-540-day extension under 8 CFR 274a.13(d).
* Extensions provided by law or by a TPS Federal Register notice are not affected.
* DHS estimates that 20,000 to 33,000 EADs could lapse in an 18-month window starting in May 2027.

## What the rule changes

Before the interim final rule, many renewal applicants could keep working after their EAD expired, as long as they had filed a timely renewal in the same category. The extension was 180 days from 2016 and was raised to up to 540 days in 2022, 2024 and by a December 2024 final rule.

The new paragraph 8 CFR 274a.13(e) says that, unless the law, paragraph (d) or a TPS Federal Register notice provides otherwise, a renewal filed on or after October 30, 2025 does not extend an expired or expiring EAD. The final rule keeps this text unchanged after reviewing 653 public comment submissions.

## Who is affected

DHS gives two examples in the rule:

* **Employment authorized incident to status** (for example an L-2 spouse): the EAD card expires on its printed date, but the person stays authorized to work while the status lasts. The expired card simply stops being valid proof.
* **Employment authorization granted by USCIS** (for example a pending adjustment of status, C09, or a pending asylum application, C08): if the renewal is still pending the day after the EAD end date, the person cannot keep working until the new EAD is approved, unless they have another basis to work.

The C08 and C09 categories made up about 80% of Form I-765 applicants from January 2025 to June 2026, according to DHS.

## What DHS expects

DHS estimates that 20,000 to 33,000 EADs may lapse in a single 18-month window starting in May 2027, and that 1,700 to 2,800 employers could be affected. It projects $312.1 million in labor turnover costs to U.S. employers and $138.1 million in lost productivity over that period, while noting that these estimates vary widely.

## Practical steps for workers and employers

1. Check the receipt date on your Form I-797C. If it is before October 30, 2025, the old extension rules can still apply.
2. File renewals as early as the Form I-765 instructions allow. DHS itself warns that the later you file, the more likely a lapse is.
3. Employers should track EAD end dates in their Form I-9 records and plan for a possible gap.
4. Holders of other visas should read our explainer on the [proposed H-1B 60-day grace period rule](/blog/h1b-60-day-grace-period-proposed-rule-2026/), and travelers to Europe our note on [ETIAS and EES in 2026](/blog/etias-ees-2026-visa-free-travel-europe/).

## FAQ

### Do I still get the 540-day automatic extension?

Only if your renewal EAD application was properly filed before October 30, 2025 and you meet the other conditions of 8 CFR 274a.13(d). Renewals filed on or after October 30, 2025 get no automatic extension.

### When did the final rule take effect?

The final rule was published in the Federal Register and took effect on October 14, 2026. It adopts without changes the interim final rule of October 30, 2025.

### Are TPS-related EADs affected?

The rule does not change automatic extensions provided by law or through a Federal Register notice for TPS-related employment documentation.

### What if I work incident to status, like an L-2 spouse?

DHS explains that the EAD document expires on its printed date, but employment authorization incident to status continues as long as the status itself lasts.

### How can I reduce the risk of a gap?

File the renewal as early as the Form I-765 instructions allow. DHS notes that the longer you wait to file, the more likely a temporary lapse becomes, because without a decision by the expiry date you cannot keep working on that EAD.
